# Judicial phrasebank

Drawn from a corpus of High Court (2024-26), Queensland Court of Appeal and Supreme Court, New South Wales Court of Appeal and Victorian Court of Appeal judgments (2025-26). Frequencies are occurrences per 10,000 words in the High Court corpus unless marked QSC. Where a phrase is marked (0) it does not appear at all and should not be used.

**Using this for client correspondence:** most of these phrases carry straight into client letters and advice and are the plain-English legal register to reach for. The exceptions are the court-only items, which read as jargon or as deciding the client's case: keep "this Court", "their Honours", "the primary judge", "the appeal must be dismissed", "the impugned decision", "the plurality", "constructional choice", "enlivened" out of a client document. Where a judgment says "that submission must be rejected", a client letter says "that argument is unlikely to succeed"; where a judgment disposes, a client letter recommends. See SKILL.md for the full translation table. See corpus-stats.md for the per-court counts and the New South Wales and Victorian variations (Victoria writes "we" and "the judge" and uses single quotes; NSW tracks the Queensland appellate register).

## Punctuation habits

- Semicolons 23 per 10,000 words (HCA), 40 (QSC): used to hold parallel items in a list, not to join two sentences for variety.
- Colons 25 (HCA), 38 (QSC): introduce quotations, lists, and the answer to a framed question ("The issue is readily resolved: plainly it is properly so characterised.").
- Parentheses 163 (HCA): defined terms, cross-references, short asides. The parenthesis is the judicial aside; the em dash is not (0 in HCA, 14 in QSC as en dashes).
- Double quotation marks (HCA) or single (Qld). Quotation marks also flag a term of art being examined: the word "loan"; "reasonably practicable".
- Question marks 4 to 7 per 10,000 words, almost all in framed issues or quoted evidence.
- Exclamation marks: only inside quoted evidence.
- Contractions: only inside quoted evidence (0.2 to 0.8 per 10,000 words).
