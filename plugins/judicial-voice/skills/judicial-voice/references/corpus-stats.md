## What the numbers say

**Sentence length.** High Court prose is long by design: a third of sentences run past 40 words. But the median is 31, not 60, and one sentence in sixteen is under ten words. Queensland judges write shorter: the Court of Appeal's median sentence is 22 words and one in eight sentences is under ten. The High Court's length comes from subordination and enumeration inside a single grammatical spine, not from chaining clauses.

**Voice.** Joint High Court reasons are almost impersonal (6 "I" per 10,000 words, most of them in separate concurrences). Queensland single judges use "I" seven times as often, but nearly all instances are "I accept", "I find", "I am satisfied", "I do not accept", "I am not persuaded", "I consider". The verb list is closed.

**Passive.** About 150 to 160 passive-type constructions per 10,000 words in every court. That is moderate, not heavy; roughly one in every four sentences. The passive is used for the institutional act ("it was submitted", "the appeal was dismissed", "was convicted") and the active for reasoning ("The submission must be rejected" is passive in form but decisive in effect).

**Punctuation.** The High Court corpus contains no em or en dashes at all. Parentheses do the work of the aside. Colons introduce quotations and answers. Semicolons hold lists together.

**New South Wales** sits between the High Court and Queensland: median sentence 28 words, "I" at 30 per 10,000 in single-author reasons, the same closed verb list, and the heaviest use of "the primary judge" and "his Honour"/"her Honour" of any court (it decides more appeals on the primary judge's findings). Its modal and rejection vocabulary matches Queensland's ("must be rejected", "no error is shown", "the submission is not to the point"). Treat NSW as the Queensland appellate register.

**Victoria** is the one court with its own fingerprint. Its "we" rate (17 per 10,000) is higher than its "I" rate (18) because a two- or three-judge bench writes in the plural; every other court is overwhelmingly "I" or impersonal. It almost never uses double quotation marks (4 per 10,000, against 56 to 287 elsewhere): Victoria uses single quotes. It uses the most dashes (23 per 10,000). And it says "the judge" where the others say "the primary judge" (1.3 per 10,000, a tenth of the others' rate). Everything else, the modal ladder, the concede-then-pivot, the rejection formulae, "for these reasons", is shared. When writing Victorian, switch three things: "we" for a bench, single quotes, and "the judge".

## Refreshing the corpus

The harvest and analysis scripts are in `scripts/` (see `scripts/README.md`). `harvest_hca.py` reads the High Court's judgments listing (Drupal, `?page=N`), follows each judgment page to its PDF and converts with pdftotext. `harvest_qld.py` walks `queenslandjudgments.com.au/caselaw/{qsc|qca}/{year}/{n}` until eight consecutive misses. `harvest_nsw.py` uses NSW Caselaw's server-rendered search to list Court of Appeal decisions, then fetches each `/decision/<id>` page. All three use plain HTTP, which is sufficient because those sites serve judgment pages server-side (only the browse/listing pages are JavaScript-driven).

`harvest_vsca.sh` fetches the Victorian Court of Appeal from AustLII, which blocks scripted HTTP behind Cloudflare. It drives the authenticated **Firecrawl** CLI, one judgment at a time, capturing stdout, paced under the free-tier ~10 requests/minute cap. Firecrawl is the official Claude Code plugin plus `firecrawl-cli`; authenticate once with `firecrawl login`, and `firecrawl --status` shows remaining credits. To extend the corpus to other AustLII courts (Federal Court, WA, SA, Tasmania), copy `harvest_vsca.sh` and change the court path.

`analyse.py` produces `stats.md`; `excerpts.py` pulls candidate paragraphs by rhetorical move for curation.
